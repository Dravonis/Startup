# Jaipur Commercial Exchange (JCE)
## Landing Page Structure & Design Specification

**Document type:** Product and UI/UX specification  
**Product:** Jaipur Commercial Exchange (JCE)  
**Initial market:** Jaipur, Rajasthan, India  
**Primary audience:** Commercial real estate brokers and agents  
**Document status:** Proposed landing-page specification for the MVP  
**Version:** 1.0

---

## 1. Purpose and Objectives

The JCE landing page should introduce Jaipur Commercial Exchange as a broker-focused network for commercial real estate professionals in Jaipur. It should explain the value of sharing commercial property inventory, posting buyer and tenant requirements, discovering potential matches, and developing co-broking relationships.

The initial landing page is intended to generate qualified broker interest—not to imply that a complete public property marketplace already exists.

### Primary objectives

1. Explain what JCE is and who it serves.
2. Communicate the problems it aims to solve.
3. Explain the basic share → match → connect workflow.
4. Establish credibility through clear, accurate messaging.
5. Encourage eligible commercial brokers to request early access or join the network.
6. Prepare visitors for the future web application without promising features that are not yet available.

### Primary conversion

**Request Early Access** or **Join the Network**.

Use **Request Early Access** if access is still being granted manually or the platform is not yet operational. Use **Join the Network** only when the registration and onboarding process is ready.

### Success measures

Track the following after launch:

- Landing-page visits.
- Primary CTA click-through rate.
- Completed early-access forms.
- Percentage of applicants who match the target broker profile.
- Application-to-onboarding conversion rate.
- Traffic source and referral source.

Do not use registrations alone as proof of product-market fit. Track whether onboarded brokers contribute genuine inventory or requirements and receive useful introductions.

## 2. Audience and Positioning

### Primary audience

Commercial real estate brokers and agents operating in Jaipur who handle one or more of the following:

- Office spaces.
- Retail shops and showrooms.
- Warehouses and logistics properties.
- Industrial properties and plots.
- Commercial land.
- Income-generating commercial assets.

### Core value proposition

> **JCE helps Jaipur's commercial real estate brokers share inventory, discover potentially matching requirements, and build co-broking connections through a focused local network.**

This is the proposed positioning. Adjust the wording to reflect the actual capabilities available at launch.

### Positioning principles

- **Commercial-first:** Do not position JCE as a general residential property portal.
- **Broker-first:** Lead with the needs of commercial property professionals.
- **Jaipur-focused:** Start with the intended local market rather than claiming nationwide coverage.
- **Matching-oriented:** Explain the practical value of connecting requirements with available inventory.
- **Trust-conscious:** Describe verification and privacy practices accurately.
- **Early-stage honest:** Clearly distinguish available features from planned features.

### Tone of voice

Use language that is:

- Professional and direct.
- Business-oriented, not overly promotional.
- Familiar to Indian commercial property professionals.
- Specific about the workflow and benefits.
- Free of unsupported claims such as “guaranteed deals,” “instant matches,” or “the largest network.”

Communicate what JCE is, who it serves, and why a broker should care within the first screen.

**Description**

A broker-focused network for Jaipur's commercial real estate market. Share property inventory, discover potentially matching buyer and tenant requirements, and build co-broking relationships in one focused place.

“Close More Deals” is an aspirational marketing message, not a guaranteed outcome. If a more conservative launch message is preferred, use:

> Find Commercial Inventory. Discover Requirements. Build Better Broker Connections.

Do not claim automated or instant matching until the feature is implemented and tested.

Use a two-column layout on desktop:

**Left column**
- Eyebrow.
- Headline.
- Description.
- Primary and secondary CTA.
- Short supporting statement.

**Right column**
- A product UI preview showing a requirement, a potential property match, and a broker connection.

The mockup may include:

- Requirement card.
- Commercial property card.

Prefer a product interface mockup over a generic building photograph. The preview should communicate the workflow at a glance.

### Acceptance criteria

- Visitors can identify JCE as commercial real estate and broker-focused.
- Primary CTA is visible without scrolling on common desktop viewports.
- The headline and buttons remain readable on mobile.
- Mock data is labelled where necessary.
- CTA wording reflects the real launch status.

## 6. Section 3 — The Problem

### Purpose

Show that JCE understands the practical friction involved in commercial broker collaboration.

### Recommended heading

## Commercial Real Estate Runs on Relationships. Finding the Right Match Shouldn't Be Difficult.

### Recommended cards

#### 01 — Scattered Inventory

Property information can be spread across WhatsApp groups, spreadsheets, messages, and personal contacts, making it difficult to maintain a clear view of available opportunities.

#### 02 — Missed Opportunities

A broker may have an active buyer or tenant requirement but lack an easy way to discover another broker holding suitable inventory.

#### 03 — Manual Matching

Brokers may spend time searching messages and contacting multiple people to identify properties that fit a client's location, budget, size, and use requirements.

### Design

- Use three cards in a row on desktop.
- Stack cards on mobile.
- Use simple icons or restrained illustrations.
- Keep descriptions short and readable.
- Avoid unsupported numerical claims about time lost, deals missed, or revenue gained.

### Acceptance criteria

- Each card describes a recognisable workflow problem.
- The content does not exaggerate or claim measured results without evidence.
- The layout is easy to scan.

## 7. Section 4 — How JCE Works

### Purpose

Explain the product workflow quickly and simply.

### Recommended heading

## From Property Requirements to Potential Matches

### Step 1 — Share

Brokers submit a commercial property or a buyer/tenant requirement with relevant details such as locality, property type, area, budget, and transaction type.

### Step 2 — Match

JCE helps identify potentially suitable properties and requirements within the participating network. The initial matching process may be manual; describe it honestly until automated matching is available.

### Step 3 — Connect

Brokers review the potential opportunity and contact the relevant broker to discuss suitability and co-broking terms.

### Design

- Three numbered steps on desktop.
- A vertical sequence on mobile.
- Use a clear visual progression: Share → Match → Connect.
- Optionally include a small UI preview beneath the steps.

### Important product distinction

Do not imply that JCE guarantees a match or closes transactions. A match is a lead for broker review, not a guarantee of availability, suitability, or transaction completion.

### Acceptance criteria

- A new visitor can explain the workflow after a brief scan.
- Each step describes a concrete action.
- Claims match the actual MVP process.

## 9. Section 6 — Built for Commercial Brokers

Differentiate JCE from general property classifieds and explain the value of joining the network.

Use three feature cards with clear titles, concise copy, and simple icons. Avoid repeating the exact wording used in the “Problem” section.

### Acceptance criteria

- Benefits describe outcomes, not just software features.
- The section explains why a broker might join.
- No unsupported claim of exclusive inventory, guaranteed deals, or universal verification is made.

## 12. Section 9 — Trust and Verification

### Purpose

Address concerns about broker identity, inventory accuracy, contact details, and co-broking expectations.

### Recommended heading

## Professional Connections Start with Trust.

### Trust principles to communicate

#### Broker checks

Explain which details are checked during onboarding. If registration details are checked, specify the scope and do not imply that every broker or property has been independently verified unless that is true.

#### Inventory clarity

Encourage brokers to provide accurate property details and update availability when circumstances change.

#### Controlled information sharing

Explain which details are visible to other members, which require permission, and how contact information is handled.

#### Reporting and moderation

Provide a clear way to report suspicious profiles, misleading inventory, or inappropriate behaviour. Publish a process only when there is a real process behind it.

#### Co-broking expectations

Clarify that brokers must agree on fees, responsibilities, client ownership, and other commercial terms directly before proceeding.

### Design

Use a concise set of trust cards or a short explanatory panel. Link to detailed policies when those pages are available.

### Acceptance criteria

- Verification language accurately reflects the checks performed.
- Privacy and data-handling statements match the implemented system.
- Reporting channels are functional before they are advertised.
- No guarantee of transaction safety or deal completion is implied.

## 15. Visual Design System

The landing page should feel like a premium B2B software product for commercial real estate professionals, not a generic residential classifieds portal.

### Suggested visual direction

| Element | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| Primary colour | Deep navy |
| Accent colour | Teal or emerald |
| Main background | White or very light grey |
| Typography | Modern, readable sans-serif |
| Headings | Strong hierarchy and concise wording |
| Cards | Subtle borders, restrained shadows |
| Buttons | High-contrast primary action |
| Imagery | Offices, retail, warehouses, industrial spaces |
| UI previews | Property cards, requirement cards, matching interface |

These are starting recommendations. Confirm the palette and typography with a small visual prototype before applying them site-wide.

### Layout principles

- Use consistent content widths and spacing.
- Keep sections visually distinct without excessive decoration.
- Use a consistent radius and shadow system.
- Avoid excessive gradients, animations, and decorative effects.
- Ensure primary buttons are easy to identify.
- Use responsive image sizes and optimised assets.
- Prefer real product UI over generic stock imagery when the interface is available.

### Responsive behaviour

**Desktop**
- Two-column hero.
- Three-column problem and benefit cards.
- Horizontal three-step workflow.
- Multi-column property-category grid.

**Tablet**
- Reduce grid columns where needed.
- Keep heading line lengths comfortable.
- Preserve the primary CTA's prominence.

**Mobile**
- Stack the hero content.
- Keep the primary CTA easy to reach.
- Use one- or two-column cards depending on width.
- Avoid horizontal overflow.
- Use comfortable touch targets.
- Test the form with mobile keyboards and validation messages.

### Accessibility

- Maintain sufficient text and button contrast.
- Use semantic HTML headings in logical order.
- Provide alt text for meaningful images.
- Ensure keyboard navigation and visible focus states.
- Associate form labels with their inputs.
- Do not rely on colour alone to communicate status.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences.

## 16. Copy and Product-Claim Rules

Because JCE is an early-stage product, all public claims should be carefully controlled.

### Safe messaging

Use wording such as:

- “Discover potential matches.”
- “Connect with commercial brokers.”
- “Share property inventory and requirements.”
- “Built for Jaipur's commercial real estate market.”
- “Request early access.”

### Avoid unless demonstrably true

- “Guaranteed deals.”
- “Instant matching across Jaipur.”
- “10,000 verified brokers on the platform.”
- “Every listing is verified.”
- “Jaipur's largest commercial network.”
- Specific conversion, revenue, or time-saving claims without supporting evidence.
- “AI-powered” if the relevant AI functionality is not implemented.

### Product-status language

Clearly distinguish:

- **Available now:** working features users can access.
- **Pilot:** features available to a limited group.
- **Coming soon:** planned features not yet available.

A design mockup is acceptable, but it must not mislead visitors into thinking a non-functional dashboard or sample inventory is live.

## 17. Recommended MVP Scope

The landing page should not force the team to build the entire marketplace before launch.

### Required for the first landing-page release

- Responsive landing page.
- JCE logo or wordmark.
- Clear broker-focused hero section.
- Problem and workflow sections.
- Commercial property categories.
- Broker-focused benefits.
- Honest product preview.
- Early-access form.
- Form submission storage or a reliable notification workflow.
- Privacy notice and contact details.
- Working navigation and CTA.
- Basic analytics and error monitoring.

### Can be added later

- Live property search.
- Public property listing pages.
- Interactive map.
- Broker directory.
- Live match dashboard.
- Automated matching engine.
- Testimonials and case studies, once genuine.
- Detailed analytics.
- Subscription pricing.

### Recommended principle

Build only the functionality needed to validate broker demand and collect qualified early-access applications. Add marketplace functionality when there is sufficient real inventory and a clear user need.

## 20. Final Recommendation

Design the landing page around one clear message:

> **JCE is a Jaipur-focused commercial real estate network that helps brokers share inventory, discover potential matches, and connect for co-broking opportunities.**

Prioritise the broker network and matching workflow over a large public property search interface. Use a strong hero, a convincing product preview, clear trust practices, and a straightforward early-access CTA.

Most importantly, build credibility by being precise about what exists today, what is being piloted, and what is planned. The landing page should help recruit the first qualified brokers and validate the network—not merely look like a fully established marketplace.
